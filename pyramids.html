SIX STRUCTURE, SIX CENTURIES

THE PYRAMIDS

From Djoser,s experimental steps to the smooth, star-aligned faces of Giza — the evolution of the pyramid form. told through six monuments.

GIZA PLATEAU • C. 2670 BCE
STEP PYRAMID OF DJOSER

The world's first monumenteal stone building — six mastabas stacked by the architect IMhotep, the pyramid's true origin.

DAHSHUR • C. 2600 BCE
BENT PYRAMID

Its angle changes mid-build, from 54° to 43° — physical evidence of Egypt's engineers correcting a structural mistake in real time.

DAHSHUR • C. 2590 BCE
RED PYRAMID

Named for the rust-tond limestone beneath its casing, this is the first successful true (smooth-sided) pyramid ever completed.

GIZA PLATEAU • C. 2570 BCE
PYRAMID OF KHAFRE

Appears taller than Khufu's due to higher ground and a steeper angle — still wears a cap of original casing stone at its peak.

GIZA PLATEAU • C. 2560 BCE
GREAT PYRAMID OF GIZA

Built for Khufu, the tallest structure on Earth for almost 3,800 years — amd still the largest pyramid by volume.

GIZA PLATEAU • C. 2510 BCE
PYRAMID OF MENKAURE

The smallest of the Giza three, faced party in red granite — later damage by an unfinished attempt to dismantle it.

Pyramid of Khafre

Khafre, Khufu's son, built the second Giza pyramid on higher bedrock at a steeper angle, making it look taller than its neighbor despite being slightly shorter. It is also linked to the Great Sphinx nearby.

Built forKhafre (4th Dynasty)
Completedc. 2570 BCE
Original height143.5 m
Base length215.3 m per side
Notable featureRetains original casing at its apex

Pyramid of Menkaure

The smallest of the three Giza pyramids, built for Khafre's son Menkaure. Its lower courses were faced in red granite rather than limestone — an unusually costly choice.

Built forMenkaure (4th Dynasty)
Completedc. 2510 BCE
Original height65 m
Base length102.2 m per side
Notable featureDamaged by a 12th-century dismantling attempt

Step Pyramid of Djoser

Designed by the architect-official Imhotep for King Djoser, this is the earliest large-scale cut-stone building known — six mastaba-like tiers stacked to form a staircase toward the sky.

Built forDjoser (3rd Dynasty)
Completedc. 2670 BCE
Original height62.5 m
LocationSaqqara necropolis
ArchitectImhotep

Bent Pyramid

Built for Sneferu, this pyramid's angle shifts partway up from a steep 54° to a shallower 43° — most likely a correction made after cracking suggested the original angle was unstable.

Built forSneferu (4th Dynasty)
Completedc. 2600 BCE
Original height105 m
LocationDahshur
Notale featureBest-preserved original outer casing of any pyramid

Red Pyramid

Also built for Sneferu, and completed after the Bent Pyramid, this is considered the world's first successful true pyramid — smooth-sided from base to apex at a stable 43° angle.

Built forSneferu (4th Dynasty)
Completedc. 2590 BCE
Height105 m
LocationDahshur
Notable featureNamed for the reddish limestone beneath its casing

Great Pyramid of Giza

Built as the tomb of Pharaoh Khufu, this was the tallest man-made structure in the world for roughly 3,800 years — a record unbroken until Lincoln Cathedral's spire in the 14th century CE.

Built forKhufu (4th Dynasty)
Completedc. 2560 BCE
Original height146.6 m
Base length230.3 m per side
Estimated blocks~2.3 million
